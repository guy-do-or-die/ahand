# The app

```
/31337/14?d=AgFP…#<link>   →   the fragment is read once into memory, then removed from the address bar
                               the Deal card: "Someone raised a hand:" over the title "a sublet in Yerevan, June."
                               [🤝 Pass it on]   [🙌 I can help]
```

`apps/web` (`@ahand/web`, AGPL-3.0-only): React 19, Vite 8, wagmi 3 (EIP-6963 injected wallets, and logging in through Privy when the configuration has `privy`; see "Logging in through Privy" below), a 60-line router over `pathname` (`src/router.ts`, one table in `src/routes.ts`). The build is a folder of static files plus a self-contained `exit.html`. English and Russian; Russian speaks as «ты». Built with `bun run --cwd apps/web build`; unit tests `cd apps/web && bun test`; browser smokes `tests/e2e/smoke/*.spec.ts`.

## Screens

| Route | Screen (`src/screens/…`) | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | `landing` | the hero, the one way in (raise a hand), an example Hand as its receiver sees it, four gesture cards that turn over to Core's calls under the lens, and one quiet question that opens a box to paste an address, bare link or QR digits. With a fragment it is the Hand view |
| `/<chain>/<hand>`, `/<chain>/<core>/<hand>` (`?d=` optional) | `hand` | the Deal card, the route as a rail of cards that turn over (the twist), "Pass it on", "I can help"; after the end "Send it back"; on a closed Hand a bounded look for the asker's newer Hand, by Hand numbers |

The app has no "What it can't promise" screen. Since 2026-11-22 the `/limits` screen, its catalogue, its footer link and the links to it (the landing's "What it can't promise →", the link after the issuer line under the reward on `/raise`, "In plain words" on `/core`, the link that was `/waiting`'s issuer line) are removed. The landing keeps the sentence "Thanking is voluntary: the asker decides." without a link; `/waiting`'s issuer sentence stays under the lens as plain text; `/core` still lists the twelve accepted limits and says "Unaudited" in full. An old `/limits` link gets no redirect: no route takes the path, so the router shows the not-found page ("Nothing here." and a link to the start), the same as any address the site does not have. A redirect to `/core` was not chosen: `/core` speaks Core's specialist words, not the plain sentences the old link promised, and a route kept only to forward would be one more path to keep. Tested by `tests/e2e/smoke/about.spec.ts`.

The shell (`src/shell/`) holds the header (the lockup, the language switch and the viewer's own button), the footer, and on a local build the ribbon `LOCAL TEST NETWORK · TEST DOLLARS`.

### Getting around

There is no menu of sections: most people arrive by a link at one screen and do one thing there. Getting around has three layers. A long screen is not laid out as one sheet: it stands in logical sections with a way to move between them from anywhere. And whoever is logged in can reach, at any moment and from any screen, their address and the money at it, their settings, their own hands and the way out, in a frame that serves any other person too.

- **The header's lockup and language** (`shell/Shell.tsx`). The lockup is drawn at 28 (24 on a phone, where the row is short), a size that holds its own beside a screen's headline. The language is one bubble with the language in use (`LangSwitch`): it opens the app's own list of all of them, each under its own name, so a third and a fourth language need a line in `LANGS` and no room in the header. The list closes on a choice, on Escape and on a press anywhere else, is walked with the arrows, and stands on the card's tone with one hairline for its edge, with no shadow.
- **The viewer in the header** (`shell/Shell.tsx` `Viewer`) stands on every screen. With an account connected it is their mark, and it opens the person sheet. With none there is nobody to draw a mark for, and a head that opened a sheet of settings told someone who came back to log in nothing: the header says "Log in" in a quiet tone, and the word opens the way in at once, over the page it stands on (`shell/person.ts` `askToEnter`); once someone is in, their sheet opens. Language stands beside it and the theme and the proof are in the footer, as on every screen. A device that passed a hand on without an account (a pass needs none) gets one quiet link, "My hands", read from the device's store after the page is drawn (under 360px the lockup, the link, the language and "Log in" do not fit one row, so the row wraps and the viewer's part stands under the lockup on the right); a hand raised here belongs to the account that raised it and is reached after logging in, so it does not bring the link. The header reads who is connected from `wallet/who.ts`, a store with no imports that the wallet layer writes to, so a page that needs no account still loads no wallet code.
- **The person sheet** (`shell/PersonSheet.tsx`, opened through `shell/person.ts`: `showMe()`, `showPerson(address, { role })`) is one frame for the viewer and for anyone else. For the viewer: the address with a copy row, the money at it (the token's `balanceOf` on every block) and what Core holds for them (`pending`, leading to /waiting), this device's raised and passed-on hands (leading to /my) and "Raise a hand", this device's settings (language, theme, the proof), and the way out. For anyone else: their mark, a proved name or "Someone", how this screen knows them, the address with the chain's own page for it, and the sentence that aHand knows a person by their address and nothing else. A section with nothing behind it is not drawn: notifications and what others said about a person (the signals contract) get theirs when they exist. The sheet is a layer (`ui/Sheet`), not a place: it changes no address and no history, so a link held in the tab and a half-written form stay. The word "wallet" is not in it; the section is "Money".
- **A person's mark** (`ui/PersonDisc.tsx`) is a five-by-five pattern drawn from the address, mirrored left to right, in one colour. It tells two "someone"s apart in the calm view, where an address is not shown; it is not proof of who someone is.
- **A long screen in sections** (`ui/SectionStrip.tsx`, first on /raise): a strip that stays at the window's top, names the screen's sections with what each is set to now ("Reward $20", "Open until 6 Feb", "Who can see it: Unlisted") and moves to one when pressed. The move is a scroll and a focus and never an address: the part after `#` can carry a link's secret here, so the items are buttons and nothing is written to the address or to history. On a phone /raise's one action stays at the bottom of the window while its sections scroll and rests in its own place at the screen's end; from 1100 px the example of what the receiver will see and the action stand in a second column that stays in view.
- **One section at a time** (`design/talk.css`, `.ah-sided--paged`; first on /raise). Each named section is at least as tall as the room under the strip, with its last part kept free for the pinned action, so one section stands on the screen, the strip stays at the top and the action at the bottom, and the next section begins below the screen's edge. The page still scrolls as any page does and comes to rest at a section's start only when let go near one (`scroll-snap-type: y proximity`): a section taller than the screen scrolls freely and a field with the keyboard open is not pulled away. The page's own top is a resting place too, or the header could not be reached. A short section stands in the middle of its screen. A section is a little shorter than the room it has, so the next one shows its head at the screen's foot: there is more below, and no control is needed to say so. A haze of the page's own colour lies over that foot, thicker toward the edge, so the next section's head is seen in shade, as what comes up on a drum is, and does not speak over the action or the section being read; it takes no taps. In one column the haze lies above the pinned action; on a wide screen, at the foot of the sections' column. There the examples and the action stand beside the sections, in a column never taller than the window (the examples scroll inside it with no bar drawn, their foot fading where there is more; the action keeps to the window's foot), and are not pages: the strip has no item for them (`beside`), and a section keeps to the top, level with the column beside it. The scheme is for screens where each section is one decision; a page that is read keeps the strip and scrolls plainly.
- **The landing** (`screens/landing/index.tsx`): what this is in two sentences, the one action a newcomer has with one line under it (the reward is paid only on thanks), and a Hand as the person it reaches sees it: the receiver's card with an example's words and amounts, with no date and nothing to press. It stands beside the words from 1000px and after the action on a phone, so the page shows the thing and does not only describe it. The example is one of a set (`screens/landing/examples.ts`, shown by `Sample.tsx`), one at a time, each the receiver's real card drawn as a picture; see "The landing's examples" below. Then the four gestures a Hand is made of (ask, pass on, help, thank): one short title each, with no full stop, and under it one quiet line with what a newcomer asks next (how long the reward waits, that passing needs no account, who reads an answer, what happens without thanks). The mechanism is told once, by the example and the cards, and not a second time in a paragraph over them. The raised hand stands on the action and on the first card, and not a third time over the headline; the example carries the card's own small still mark. Under the lens the cards stand two in a row, so Core's call on a card's back is not broken mid-name, and a card is as tall as its taller side whichever is up, so turning one moves nothing. One's own hands are behind the header's button and "Do it without us" is in the footer on every screen, so the landing repeats neither. Someone who holds a link opens it as any link; the box to paste one is folded into one question after the gestures, for where there is no address bar.
- **The landing's examples** (`screens/landing/examples.ts`, `Sample.tsx`; the asker's words are `landing.example.<id>.title` and `.details` in the landing's catalogues). One Hand cannot show what the thing is for, so the example is one of eighteen that differ on three things at once: the kind of need (a person or an introduction, a place to live, a rare thing or a repair, a specialist, something lost, a job or a hire, what only a local knows, a favour that cannot wait, what only a friend of a friend still has), the size of the reward (none, a few dollars, tens, hundreds, thousands) and the shape of the route (no pass, one pass that kept nothing, a few passes with different shares, a dozen and more, where "17 people passed it on" is still one line). `examples.ts` holds what has no language (the reward and what each pass kept, each share a whole number of Core's basis points of the reward, so every amount is one a real Hand could show); the words are in the catalogues, so they are typed for parity, read by the words guards, and fetched in the reader's language only.
  - **What is in the set.** A need is there only if it travels from person to person and paying on thanks suits it: the answer is with someone the asker cannot find alone, a friend of a friend can point at it, and the asker can tell whether it helped. Left out: what a search or a marketplace does better (a thing sold in any shop, a taxi, a plumber for today), what it is unlawful or unsafe to reward (a medical referral paid by the one referred to, blood and organs, an introduction to an official, a witness, an investor or a buyer of securities, a place in a queue, finding a person who has not asked to be found), and what invites fraud (a reward paid before the thing is back, votes, reviews, followers). A search for a person is there only where the person found gains by it and answers themself. The places are from all over the world and ones most people have heard of. No example names a brand or a real person, mentions money in its words, or carries a date.
  - **How they are shown.** One at a time, in the place of one card. Every example lies in the same cell and only the one in turn is seen, so the place is as tall as the tallest at any width and in either language and going to another moves nothing on the page (the gesture cards under the lens hold their height the same way). The next one comes by itself after nine seconds (`STANDS_MS`), arriving the way a Hand does: down its route, beat after beat, who asked, then those who passed it on, then the reader's own place, and the asker's hand greets once, as it does on the receiver's card. The example the page opens with stands still. It waits while the pointer is over the example or the focus is in it, on a page nobody sees, and for as long as the reader has paused it. Where the device asks for less motion the next one still comes in its time, by tone alone: each beat appears in the same order without rising and no hand greets (the app's rule, the tone stays and every move stops, `design/tokens.css`), so the pause is there on every device. The picture is the receiver's card and nothing else, so what is about it stands in the caption's line: what it is ("What your people see"), the pause (a bare glyph, as the footer's switches are) and the quiet control that brings the next at once ("Another example", written as the page's other quiet question is). The order is fixed: the first is the plain case (tens of dollars, two passes that each kept a share), and no two neighbours share a kind or a route's length. A screen reader is told the words of an example the reader asked for, and never of one that came by itself.
  - **Drawn by the card itself.** Each example is `DealPicture` (`ui/DealCard.tsx`): the receiver's own card of an open Hand, drawn by the same component and so by the same rules. The whole reward stands at the asker only when passes kept some of it. A pass that kept nothing has no line about keeping. A Hand with no reward says so where the amount would stand. A picture has no clock and nothing to press: no date and no row that points at one, the words stand whole with no "Read all", the title is not a heading of the page it illustrates, and the lens does not turn it, since there is no chain behind it to show. The line that counts the passes does not open: opened, seventeen rows would push the page the example is held still on, and every row would say "someone". The landing draws no beat of its own and its stylesheet only times a beat's arrival, which `examples.test.tsx` holds, together with each picture being the real card of the same Hand less its date, in both languages, and the real card finding nothing in the words to warn about.
  - **The receiver's two actions are not drawn.** The page has one action, the reader of the landing is the one who will ask and is shown someone else's screen ("What your people see"), and there is no Hand here to pass or to answer. A version with the two actions under the example was built and compared, and the picture was chosen.

- **/raise says one thing under each choice**: under the reward "You can add to it later." (what holds the money and what its issuer can do stand under the lens; where test money comes from is said when it runs short and in the reader's own sheet), under the term that after the date an unthanked reward can be sent back to the asker, by anyone and only to them (Core's `refund` runs when someone calls it), and that the date can be extended. The term is "Days it stays open": 7, 30 or 90 as bare numbers, and beside them the date the days come to ("until 4 Apr"), which opens the app's own month of days under the row for any other day from tomorrow to Core's cap, both by the chain's clock (`ui/DayPick.tsx`, `screens/raise/link.ts` `daysForDate`). The calendar is the app's and not the browser's date field, which is a different thing on every device and on a desktop a foreign one: a tone under it, the chosen day on amber, days outside the span quiet, weeks from Monday, the arrows moving by a day and a week across months, one day in the tab order; picking a day or pressing Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on the date, which, when it holds the choice, says so with `aria-current`. It reads no clock: the screen gives it the first day, the last and the chosen one. The question the asker answers is the first section's heading, set as every section's is, so the loud thing on the screen is what they type. Fine-tune (the helper's least share) stands folded with the reward it splits. "Who can see it" names the three modes Public, Unlisted and Hidden ("dark" in the code and the SDK; on screen that word already names the theme) and says two things in a line each: whether the Hand may travel beyond the people the link is handed to (a public Hand's words are all outside the link, so anyone may read, repost and list them; an unlisted or hidden one is listed nowhere), and what the address itself leaves open: the title and the details, the title, or nothing, not even the Hand's number. What every mode shares is said once under the three. Nothing in this build stores or lists public Hands: the card says what the asker allows. The two things a person will meet stand together in the last section, in the order they meet them: the link's preview in a chat, then the page it opens. The preview's card writes its date in UTC (a preview robot has no zone), so where the reader's own day of the expiry differs, a line in the term's section, under the date it explains, says the day the preview will show (`previewDayApart` in `screens/raise/link.ts`). It stands there and not under the preview so the column of examples on a wide screen keeps one height for a mode at any hour.
- **No semicolons in the words** (`tests/guards/punctuation.test.ts`): a sentence ends and the next begins. A list in brackets takes commas.
- **A title takes no full stop**, as a label and a button take none: a screen's headline ("Your hand is up", "Thank"), an entry's title over its description (a gesture card on the landing, an entry on /defaults). Running sentences keep theirs, and so do a headline of two sentences (the landing's "Raise a hand. Your people take it from there."), a sentence inside a card ("Time's up.") and every error.
- **A Hand's card is the route's spine** (`ui/DealCard.tsx`, `ui/Spine.tsx`, `design/talk.css`; "Conversation" is a metaphor to steer by, not a promise of a chat). In the calm view, on every screen that shows a Hand, the card reads top to bottom in beats joined by one hairline, each beat's mark a system emoji standing on the page with no plate under it:
  - **who asked**: the line that says so ("Someone raised a hand:", or "You raised a hand:" on the asker's own screens; in the Hand view it opens the asker's sheet), the asker's words in the reader's device font with no bubble around them, then the whole reward and the date in one quiet line;
  - **who passed it on**, when the screen knows the route (the Hand view, /pass, /give): one line whatever their number ("33 people passed it on"; Core accepts hundreds of passes and in the calm view a pass has no name) with what they kept together under it. The line opens down the spine, one small row for each pass in the route's order: who (a proved name, or "someone"; where the pass named an address, the word opens that person's sheet) and what that pass kept, by Core's own sum (`marginOf`). A pass names where its share lands and nothing more, so a row is never a claim about who held the link. A route longer than forty passes lists the forty nearest to the viewer and counts the earlier ones. A pass that named the asker's own address is said on this row without opening anything;
  - **who answered**, on a link that carries an answer;
  - **the last beat**: on a Hand that can still be passed or answered by whoever reads, their own place, "It reached you", with what reaches them on amber under its condition (when the card knows no route, what was kept is said beside the amount: "of $60: $3 was kept along the way"); on a Hand that is over, what it came to ("Time's up.", "Thanked on 3 Dec."). The public view, where the reader has no place on the route, and the asker's own card end with the asker's beat; the asker's card then says the floor they set and what passing on and nobody being thanked mean, as rows.
  Each fact stands once. The whole reward is said at the asker unless the reader's own beat shows that very amount (nothing was kept on the way). The date stands at the asker, followed by how far off it is by the chain's clock, counted as a person counts it ("open until 31 Jan, in 1 month and 7 days"; `ui/format.ts` `span`), and the row about nobody being thanked points back at it ("by then").
  The same card can be drawn as a picture of a Hand that is on no chain (`DealPicture`, for the landing's examples): the same beats with no date, no row that points at one and nothing to press.
  So each amount stands with whose it is, and the reward less what was kept is the amount on amber. There is no empty place for "the next person": "Pass it on" makes it. Under the lens the card is drawn in full (Core's figures, each opening to its source) and the screen draws the rail, whose hands turn over; the rail is also drawn on /thank and in an answer the asker reads, with its hands standing on the page.
- **The link's preview is one card, drawn by one code** (`packages/card`: `cardModelOf`, `metaOf`, `Card`). The view server turns it into the 1200×630 PNG a messenger shows (`apps/view/src/og.tsx`, satori and resvg) and writes its title and description lines (`meta.ts`); /raise draws the very same elements in the page from the draft, scaled to the column, with those lines under it (`screens/raise/parts.tsx` `Unfurl`). Nothing is fetched there: before the raise the Hand has no number, and the card never names one (nor the chain): a reader in a chat learns nothing from it, and the address beside the picture says it. The card reads as the receiver's card does: "Someone raised a hand:", the title in the asker's words, the details when the Hand is Public, then the whole reward and the date in one quiet line with amber only under the amount, and on a Hand that is over what it came to; at the foot of an open Hand one quiet line says what opening the link is for ("Open it to help or pass it on."), words and not a button, since a picture in a chat cannot be pressed and the whole preview opens the link. It knows only what stands before `#` and the chain, so it never says what reaches the viewer. The date stands without how far off it is: a chat keeps a preview as it was fetched, and "in 5 days" would go on saying so after they passed. The image cannot use the reader's emoji or device font, so it leaves emoji out (the messenger's title line keeps them) and is set in the app's display faces in the app's own order: Schibsted Grotesk, Onest for Cyrillic, Noto Sans for other scripts. - **The way in from the sheet opens over the page.** The sheet is a modal `<dialog>`, which the browser draws above everything on the page, and Privy's window is an ordinary layer of the page: opened from inside the sheet it stood under it, unseen (seen on the public build on 2026-11-21; the stand has no Privy and could not show it). So "Log in" in the sheet closes the sheet, opens the way in over the page (`shell/person.ts` `askToEnter`, `PersonEnter`), and brings the sheet back once someone is in.
- **The gesture button** (`ui/SwipeButton.tsx`, `design/components.css` "Marker-swipe button"). Its fill fades out toward the gesture's emoji, which leans out past the button's end and stands on the page, so the button and its hand read as one thing. The fill is a layer under a mask, so its colour can change as one tone while the fade keeps its shape. There is one button: one height, one hand of one size in one place for every gesture, and one fade, wherever it stands and however wide it is; there is no compact, hero or card button, and a phone has the same two actions a wide screen has. The fade is one S-curve, and every anchor of it is a place on the hand (`--hand-size`, `--hand-right`, from which the hand itself is drawn, centred on a box of one width whatever the emoji font), so it is the same on every button. Each fill has its own rule: a neutral fill (ink, or paper in the dark theme) greys as it thins, so its fade begins nearly two hands back, long enough to be seen as a fade and not as an edge, and is gone under the hand's middle; amber thins cleanly but the yellow hand blends with it, so it thins from three hands back, 22% is left where the hand begins, and the rest goes under the hand. On a button too narrow for the full fade, the fade is shorter and begins where the room a label has ends (`--label-room`, 150px from the button's left edge: the padding and about 128px of words), so a label of that length is still read on the solid fill. That holds from about 186px of width for the neutral fill and 230px for amber: narrower still, a fade keeps a small length of its own and begins inside that room, and a label longer than the room runs past it on any button. On the Hand screen, below the two-column layout (768px), the two actions stand in a bar at the foot of the screen: one over the other on a phone held upright (172px, where the screen is at least 560px tall), side by side from 600px of width (one row, 88px, a phone held sideways). On a screen both narrow and short there is no bar and the two buttons stand in the page. A keyboard has the same outcome by another check: the screen watches for typing and for the visual viewport, since on iOS the height a media query sees does not shrink when a keyboard opens. The room kept at the foot for a control brought into view is as tall as the bar is now, so it follows the bar when the phone is turned. The bar has no line over it: it stands on the page's colour under the same haze as the raise screen's pinned action (`.ah-bar` in `design/talk.css`), so what scrolls up from under it leaves through shade, and at the page's foot the haze lies over the room the bar keeps above itself. Before this there were four sizes of button, each with pixel anchors of its own and each gesture with a hand of its own size: the fill ended before the hand on one and under it on another, and a phone's pair had a short hard fade and a hand inside the button where a wide screen's had a long fade and a hand above it. The answer to the pointer is one step of tone for the whole fill in 160 ms with the hand leaning; the answer to a press is a deeper tone at once, the button giving 2% and the hand pushing, let go over 300 ms. A light fill has no room to get lighter, so the dark theme's paper fill steps darker. A press is held for at least 150 ms (`data-pressed`), because a finger's tap is over in a frame or two and iOS shows `:active` late. Nothing sweeps across the button. Each gesture's own move (`design/tokens.css`, `ah-raise`, `ah-shake`, `ah-cheer`, `ah-bow`) is drawn the way a hand does it, a small wind-up and swings that die down, once in a five-second round; under the pointer or a press it yields to the hand's answer, and reduced motion keeps the tone and stops every move. Tests: `apps/web/src/ui/gesture.test.tsx`, `tests/e2e/smoke/gesture.spec.ts`.
- **Colours and layout for the browsers phones still run.** An iPhone that was never updated runs Safari 16.0 or 16.1, and that Safari has no `color-mix()` and does not read a width condition written as a range. So a see-through colour is never made with `color-mix()`: every step of the ink, paper and amber ladders is `rgb(R G B / a)` over the theme's channels (`--ink-rgb`, `--paper-rgb` in `design/tokens.css`), the swipe button's fade and the haze over a pinned action are written the same way, markup uses the ladder's tokens and no utility with an opacity modifier, and a field's hint has its own token. Where `color-mix()` was used, the build left the solid colour for such a browser: quiet text turned loud, a tone turned into a slab with its words unreadable on it, and a button's fade into a hard edge. Both builds also write their stylesheets for those browsers (`cssTarget` in `vite.shared.ts`), so a width condition stays `min-width` or `max-width`. Print forces ink on white, so it sets the channels with the colours, and a rule that sets `--ink` or `--paper` without its channels fails a guard. Held by `apps/web/tests/guards/colour.test.ts` (no `color-mix()` and no opacity modifier in the app, its exit page and the card package, channels equal to the raw colours and set wherever a theme colour is) and `tests/e2e/guards/old-safari.spec.ts` (each page opened as served and with `color-mix()` taken away computes the same, and no served stylesheet has a range condition). The same holds for script: the app's own code, its exit page and the workspace packages call nothing newer than Safari 15.4. The code that follows a sent transaction used `AbortSignal.any` (Safari 17.4), and on every older iPhone it threw right after the transaction had gone out: a raise in two steps sent the allowance and never the raise, and any other send reached the chain while its screen never said so. The two signals are joined by one controller now, in `lib/abort.ts` (`stopWith`). Held by `apps/web/tests/guards/browser.test.ts` (the sources) and `apps/web/tests/setup/old-browser.ts`, which takes the newer functions away from every unit test started in `apps/web` (the packages' own suites run in their folders with a full runtime: what the app ships of them is read as source by the guard and run by the app's tests that import them); what a build writes is read in `apps/web/tests/vercel.test.ts` (no `color-mix()` but the reset's hint colour under its own `@supports`, no range condition, in the stylesheets and in exit.html). Tailwind does not read the tests (`@source not` in `design/index.css`), so a guard's sample strings never become rules a visitor downloads. Not covered: what a library in `node_modules` calls on a path no unit test runs, and Safari older than 15.4.

## What the device stores

The link's 16-byte seed lives in the address its holder was sent and in the memory of the tab that opened it (`src/store/memory.ts`); a reload forgets it, and the screen says so. IndexedDB database `ahand` (`src/store/db.ts`):

| Store | Holds | A secret? |
|---|---|---|
| `books` | the 8-byte book per asker; whether the account repeats its signatures | no |
| `hands` | my Hands: chain, Core, number, `n`, the raise intent and hash, mode, the words and salt | the asker's own words; no key |
| `inbox` | answers received or pasted, as give links (no secret by format), first-seen time on this device, routes seen | no |
| `outbox` | the helper's give links, delivery status, the per-answer chat key | a chat key only |
| `passed` | Hands passed from this device: chain, number, position, payee | no |
| `prefs` | language, lens, theme; in a build with Privy, the raise form while a social login leaves the page and comes back (`raiseForm`, at most 30 minutes, below) | the asker's own words; no key, no link |
| `vault` | one AES-GCM key per device, created non-extractable | cannot be read out |
| `escrow` | **the locked copy**: for an account whose signatures do not repeat, the new Hand's seed encrypted under the vault key, written before `raise` is sent, deleted when the asker ticks "I saved the link" or the raise is found reverted, and by the sweep on every start (below) | yes, encrypted, at most one day after the link is shown |

The locked copy (`lockSeed`, `openSeed`, `dropSeed`) binds the raise intent as AES-GCM associated data with a fresh IV. It protects against a storage dump and a crash, not against this site's own code. It is a deliberate exception to keeping no secret on the device. Each copy carries two chain times (`stampSeed`): when the raise read the chain, and when the "up" screen first showed the link. On every start the app sweeps them (`apps/web/src/screens/raise/sweep.ts`, by the chain's clock, never the device's): a copy is deleted once its Hand is closed or past its end, one day (`SHOWN_BOUND`, 24 h) after its link was first shown, when its raise reverted, or when no raise on the device names it; a copy whose raise is still on its way, or whose Hand cannot be read now, is kept. Until then `/my` says the link is kept locked on this device. Tests: `apps/web/src/screens/raise/sweep.test.ts` and `apps/web/tests/guards/store.test.ts` (no seed in any store in any form; the vault key not exportable). The theme toggle also writes one `localStorage` key, `ahand-theme`.

Clearing the storage loses only "My hands on this device", answers seen and the outbox; every flow still works from a link.

A give counts as delivered by the outbox's own rule (`acksNeeded` of `@ahand/deliver`, [delivery](delivery.md)): two relays acknowledged it, or every relay when fewer are configured. Until then `/give` says "Sending it to the asker…" and `/my` "Not delivered yet", even when one relay already holds it, unless no relay is left to try; a give that waits makes every screen open the outbox at start (`stillDelivering`, `apps/web/src/screens/give/waiting.ts`). Retries start after 10 s and double up to ten minutes. An account whose keys confirmations repeat but are not its own key's signature (a smart account, a wrapped or a passkey's) gets no master; `/my` says so in its own sentence (`my.unlock.notKey`) rather than that its answers differ.

## What is logged

- **The app**: nothing leaves the device. No `console` call in `src`, no `sessionStorage` but the two keys that carry an opened link, and on /give the answer typed so far, across a social login's reload (`link-carry.ts`; see "Logging in through Privy" below), no cookies, no analytics, no service worker. React's error hooks are no-ops so an error carrying link text never reaches the console. Guards: `apps/web/tests/guards/secrets.test.ts`; `tests/e2e/guards/hygiene.spec.ts` searches every request, console line and storage entry of a run for the seed as hex, base64url and decimal digits.
- **The view server** (`apps/view`) and **`infra/stand/static.ts`**: method, path without the query, status. Never a fragment, never `d`.
- **The stand's relay** (`infra/relay/relay.ts`): counts only (stored, accepted, rejected, clients).

## Headers

The view server and `static.ts` send `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; … connect-src 'self' <rpc> <relay>; frame-ancestors 'none'` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` (`apps/view/src/headers.ts`). `exit.html` alone also allows any RPC the reader types, and the hash of its one inline script. No third-party script from another origin.

A build whose configuration has `privy` adds what Privy's [Content Security Policy page](https://docs.privy.io/security/implementation-guide/content-security-policy) (read 2026-11-21) lists for the ways in this app offers, and nothing else (`apps/web/csp.ts` `PRIVY_CSP`, the same in the view server's `PRIVY_SOURCES`, `VIEW_PRIVY=1`):

- `frame-src` and `child-src`: `https://auth.privy.io` (Privy's login and key frame), `https://verify.walletconnect.com` and its fallback `https://verify.walletconnect.org` (WalletConnect's verify frame);
- `connect-src`: `https://auth.privy.io` (Privy's API), `https://*.rpc.privy.systems` (the RPC Privy's wallets send through), `wss://relay.walletconnect.com` and `wss://relay.walletconnect.org` (WalletConnect's relay, for a phone app), `wss://www.walletlink.org` (Coinbase's relay, which the page lists among the required domains of `@privy-io/react-auth`), `https://explorer-api.walletconnect.com` (WalletConnect's list of wallets);
- `img-src`: `https://explorer-api.walletconnect.com`, the icons of that list (Privy's page keeps `img-src 'self' data: blob:` and lists no image host; the icons come from there, seen on the built page, so they are named).

Where the app's policy differs from Privy's page on purpose: `img-src` has no `blob:` (in Privy's 3.52.0 code, as in 3.51.3, only the wallet-password form, which this app does not use, and the cards kit make `blob:` URLs), `worker-src` stays `'none'` where Privy's page has `'self'` (Privy's code on the page starts no worker), and `form-action` stays `'none'` where Privy's page has `'self'` (no form here posts anywhere). Privy's own frame on `auth.privy.io` runs under Privy's own policy, which the page's does not govern. Not yet seen under a real login: the email code screen, a passkey, Farcaster and the signing of Privy's own wallet. On the first real round, keep a `securitypolicyviolation` listener on (`document.addEventListener("securitypolicyviolation", …)` in the browser's console) and add what is missing, by name.

Left out of Privy's list: Cloudflare's `script-src`/`frame-src` (Privy's bot protection stays off; turning it on in the dashboard needs a third-party script, which the app's rule of no script from another origin does not allow), Telegram, the funding kit and Solana. `script-src` stays `'self'`: Privy's code ships in the app's own files. `exit.html` never gets any of it. Measured on a Base Sepolia build served at `http://localhost:3411` (2026-11-21, after Google, Apple and X were offered everywhere and Coinbase's relay was added): Privy's window opened six times (three from "Raise your hand", three from "Log in"; English and Russian, light and dark, 1440 and 390 px), its wallet screen three times and Rainbow's WalletConnect code once, with no CSP violation on the page and no page error; Privy's own frame on `auth.privy.io` reports its own violations to Datadog under its own policy, which the page's policy does not govern.

## Logging in through Privy

One way in through Privy, with email, social accounts and wallets in the same window: where the configuration has `privy` there is one way in, Privy's own window, and nothing of the app's comes before it:

- The control is "Log in" («Войти»). It, and every action that needs an account (the raise swipe, "Log in to get paid" on /give and /pass, Deliver on /waiting, the thanks, the refund, the withdrawal), opens Privy's window directly (`apps/web/src/wallet/ConnectSheet.tsx` `PrivyLogin`). The window offers an email field, Google, X, "Other socials" (Apple, Farcaster), "Continue with a wallet" and "I have a passkey". "Continue with a wallet" lists the wallets this browser announces (EIP-6963: MetaMask, Rabby, Coinbase Wallet's extension and others), then MetaMask, Rainbow, Zerion and Uniswap by name, then WalletConnect's list for every other phone app (602 wallets on 2026-11-21). Which ways in Privy offers and how each is turned on: Privy's [login methods](https://docs.privy.io/authentication/user-authentication/login-methods/oauth) pages. The first time on a page, while Privy's code loads (1.8 to 2.8 s measured locally), a line at the bottom says "Opening log in…" («Открываем вход…»); while Privy's window is open the app shows nothing of its own.
- Google, Apple and X are offered on every screen. They leave the page: Privy's SDK sends the browser to the provider (`window.location.assign`; Privy 3.52.0, as 3.51.3, has no pop-up for them) and the provider sends it back to the same address with `privy_oauth_code`; Privy then loads at once and the person is let in. A fresh load forgets what the page holds only in memory:
  - the raise form: when the swipe asks someone to log in, the form (the words, the reward, the days, the mode, the shares) is written to `prefs` as `raiseForm`, removed however the login ends on the page, and taken out as /raise opens, filled back in only if it was written within 30 minutes (`apps/web/src/screens/raise/kept.ts`). The asker's own words, which `hands` keeps anyway once raised; no seed, no key, no link. Then the swipe again goes on logged in.
  - an opened link: carried across that one trip, the one exception to the rule that a reload forgets an opened link (since 2026-11-21). While Privy's window is open on the Hand view with a link, /give or /pass, the page leaving (`pagehide`; Privy itself starts the redirect, so that is the narrowest moment the app sees) writes the link, as memory held it, with the path and the device's time, to this tab's `sessionStorage` under `ahand-link-carry` (`apps/web/src/link-carry.ts`, armed by `openLogin` in `wallet/privy-host.tsx`). The key is erased when the login ends on the page, when the page comes back from the back-forward cache, and on every load right after link intake (`main.tsx`), before use; it is put back into memory (never into the address bar, whose query then holds Privy's one-time code) only on the way back from a social login, on the same path, within ten minutes, and when the address brought no link of its own. Storage that refuses carries nothing: the person opens the link once more, and "You're logged in. … open the one you were sent once more." (`LinkAgain`) still says so. For later, not built: a setting of the person's own, kept encrypted in ENS data records.
  - the answer typed on /give: carried with the link since 2026-11-22 (a helper who typed first and then logged in with X came back to an empty field). At the same moment, and only when the link was written, a second key, `ahand-give-draft`, holds the field's text, the Hand it was typed for (chain, Core, number) and the link record's time; an empty field writes nothing. It is erased with the link on every path, and on the way back /give fills it in once, only for that same Hand, only while memory holds the link restored with it (`takeCarriedDraft`, taken in an effect so a second render cannot take it twice). Storage that refuses it carries the link alone. The choice of where the money lands needs no carrying: back logged in, the account becomes the choice, as it does after any login on the page.
- A login that Privy answers with an error (other than the window being closed), or whose wallet never appears, ends with one line at the bottom, "Logging in didn't finish. Nothing was sent." with "Try again" (opens Privy's window again) and "Close"; never the app's own sheet. Only Privy unreachable brings the app's own sheet, below.
- The account is the wallet the person logged in with: a wallet Privy makes and keeps (`createOnLogin: users-without-wallets`, its key inside Privy's frame on `auth.privy.io`) for email, social accounts and passkeys; the crypto wallet itself for a wallet login (Privy asks it for a sign-in message first). In the app it is one wagmi connector (`apps/web/src/wallet/privy.ts`) over that wallet's EIP-1193 provider, so raise (approve, then raise, as two steps: Privy's wallet has no EIP-5792 batch), give and shake signatures (`eth_signTypedData_v4`), thanks, refunds, withdrawals and the switch to chain 84532 take the same path as before. No `@privy-io/wagmi`: the wallet layer has no React providers to adapt.
- The network: Privy's own wallet moves to Base Sepolia at once; a crypto wallet is asked to (its own window, Privy adds the chain if the wallet lacks it). If the person says no, it stays connected on its own chain and the screens' own "switch to Base Sepolia" step takes over, as for any wallet.
- Logged in, the control shows "Logged in: 0x12…34" («Вход выполнен») and "Log out" («Выйти»), which ends the Privy session on this device. Another account is "Log out", then "Log in".
- Staying logged in: on login the app writes one word, `"1"`, under the key `ahand-privy` in localStorage (`wallet/privy-mark.ts`, wrapped in try/catch; the secrets guard allows this key and the theme's only, and exempts only the mark's own unit test, which stands a Map in for localStorage). Privy's SDK writes its own keys in this site's localStorage as soon as its window opens, before anyone logs in (seen on 2026-11-21: `privy:caid`, a client id; `privy:sent:<appId>:…`; `privy:connections`), and keeps its session there once someone is in; it also asks `explorer-api.walletconnect.com` for the wallet list every time the window opens, even for someone who goes on by email. None of that happens before the first "Log in" or action, or the idle restore of a marked browser. On a later visit with the mark, Privy's code loads once the page is idle and lets the person back in without a window; if Privy finds no session, the mark is removed. Without the mark nothing of Privy loads: a first load of `/` contacted only the site itself, and the chunks of Privy's SDK are reached only through `privy-host-*.js` (`tests/vercel.test.ts`, `tests/bundle/build.test.ts`, `tests/bundle/audit.ts privySdkFiles`).
- Privy's own confirmation windows follow the lens: they show with the lens on only. With the lens on (the footer's magnifier switch, or `?`), an account Privy keeps shows Privy's own window for every signature (a Give, a shake, the keys message) and every transaction, after the app's own review step, so a person who checks sees what is signed in Privy's words. With the lens off it shows none, and the app's review step and progress rail are the confirmation; so that such an account still sees what it confirms, the give screen's agreement keeps the contract and the hand's number in the calm view for it. A crypto wallet logged in through Privy shows its own window for every request either way. How: `apps/web/src/wallet/privy-host.tsx` sets Privy's `embeddedWallets.showWalletUIs` to the lens, and its `Host` gives Privy the settings again on every flip (`lookKey` and `watchLook` include the lens, as they do the theme and the language), with Privy left mounted and the session kept (`privy-host.test.ts`, "a flip of the lens reaches Privy"). Privy 3.52.0, as 3.51.3, reads that setting as each request comes (`isHeadlessSigning`, through the wallet provider's handlers, which always call Privy's latest ones); requests through the wallet's EIP-1193 provider, which the app uses, carry no per-request `uiOptions`, so the setting is the only switch. Privy reports each set of settings it has not seen on this device once to its own analytics (`sdk_initialize`), the lens's as the theme's. Closing Privy's window answers 4001, which the app reads as "no", as for any wallet. With the lens on, Privy's transaction window does more than ask: once the person confirms, it waits for the transaction to be mined, shows "Transaction complete", and hands the hash back to the app only when the person presses "All Done" or closes it; until then the app's progress rail stays on "asking" (`wallet/send.ts`), and in a raise with an approve first Privy waits for the approve's receipt and then the app waits again. That wait cannot be turned off from the app, since the EIP-1193 path takes no `uiOptions`. Also with the lens on, an account with some ETH but too little for gas gets Privy's "missing funds" view, whose button stays disabled (Privy's funding is not on); closing it answers 4001, so the app says "you said no" rather than naming the funds (the raise screen's own check refuses only a zero balance); with the lens off the same send fails as insufficient funds. Not yet seen in a browser: no real login has been done.
- Wallets listed: `detected_ethereum_wallets`, `metamask`, `rainbow`, `zerion`, `uniswap`, `wallet_connect` (well-known names before WalletConnect's alphabetical list of hundreds). Not Coinbase's own SDK (`coinbase_wallet`, `base_account`): its smart wallet opens a pop-up that the app's `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` would cut off; its extension is announced by EIP-6963 anyway (Coinbase's relay is still allowed in `connect-src`, as Privy's page requires).
